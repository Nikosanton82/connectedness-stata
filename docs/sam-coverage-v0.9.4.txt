Monte Carlo coverage of the bootstrap intervals of the spillover asymmetry measure
(connectedness 0.9.4, estat bootstrap, sam). Produced by test/r/sam_coverage_094.R,
an independent R implementation of the joint bootstrap (own generalized
decomposition), on 25 October 2026:
    Rscript sam_coverage_094.R 500 2 500
    Rscript sam_coverage_094.R 300 2 1000

Design. Gaussian VAR(1), k = 3, no intercept, B = 0.3 I + 0.1 (J - I), residual
correlations 0.4. The positive and negative parts are max(x, 0) and min(x, 0). Since
the process is symmetric, -min(x, 0) = max(-x, 0) has the distribution of max(x, 0),
and connectedness is unchanged when every series changes sign, so the SAM and its
TO, FROM and NET are zero. Bootstrap as in estat bootstrap: VAR(1) with intercept,
horizon 10, 199 draws per round, two rounds (bias correction), percentile intervals
recentred on the bias-corrected estimate. The last row (TCI) is a benchmark: the TCI
of the total system, a correctly specified VAR, against its population value.

Reading. With T = 500 the intervals of the SAM measures cover zero about as often as
the TCI intervals of the correctly specified total system cover the true TCI (0.86
against 0.85 at 90%, 0.90 against 0.91 at 95%): the joint bootstrap carries over the
small-sample behaviour of the Greenwood-Nimmo, Kocenda and Nguyen (2024) procedure to
the SAM, which slightly undercovers. With T = 1000 the TCI intervals reach their
nominal levels (0.89 and 0.95) and those of the SAM improve (0.88 and 0.92), with a
remaining shortfall of one to three points; the positive and negative parts are not
linear VARs, so their iid residual resampling is an approximation. The estimates of
the SAM measures centre on zero, as they should.
